# Simplicity review

October 2026. Why the home page felt like "a lot going on", what the best simple sites do instead, and the 10 rules we now follow.

## What we looked at

| Site | Headline (words) | Body sections | Main button | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Apple, AirPods Pro](https://www.apple.com/airpods-pro/) | 7 | 15, but most are 3 to 10 words over a big visual | Buy | Headlines are 2 to 5 word fragments. The picture does the explaining. |
| [Stripe](https://stripe.com) | 6 | 8 | Get started | One headline, one subhead, then visuals. About half of each screen is picture. |
| [Linear](https://linear.app) | 8 | about 8 | Get started | About 300 words of body copy on the whole page. Four big product shots carry it. |
| [Superhuman](https://superhuman.com) | 8 | about 9 | Get Mail | Leads with a plain outcome: "Save 4+ hours each week". |
| [Cal.com](https://cal.com) | 7 | 10 | Get started | 3-step "how it works". The FAQ is the longest block and the weakest part. |
| [Basecamp](https://basecamp.com) | 10 | 11 | Try Basecamp free | Friendly, short sentences, roughly grade 6 to 8. Same button at top and bottom. |
| [Rosie (AI answering)](https://heyrosie.com) | 8 | about 20 | Start My Free Trial | Clear promise, but 20 sections feels long. 3-step setup works well. |
| [Goodcall (AI answering)](https://www.goodcall.com) | 7 | 13 | Build it | Best idea: an animated example call that shows the product working. |

Our home page before this change: 14 sections, about 2,200 visible words, two kinds of main button and a running ticker under the hero.

## The 10 principles

1. **Headline under 10 words.** Every site above lands its promise in 6 to 10 words. Say the result, not the tool.
2. **One line of "how" under it.** One sentence, about 20 words, that a 12-year-old could repeat back. (Stripe, Superhuman.)
3. **Six to eight sections, no more.** Stripe, Linear and Superhuman stop at about 8. Rosie at 20 feels like a list, not a story.
4. **One idea per section, 20 to 60 words.** Apple uses under 10 words per section. Linear about 20. If a section needs more, it belongs on an inner page.
5. **Show, don't tell.** The best pages are about two thirds picture by area. A moving example (Goodcall's animated call, our inbox demo) beats a paragraph about it.
6. **One main button, same words everywhere.** Basecamp says "Try Basecamp free" at the top and the bottom. Give one clear next step, plus one small quiet option.
7. **Three steps for "how it works".** Cal.com, Rosie and Goodcall all use exactly three. People remember three.
8. **Everyday words, short sentences.** Aim for a grade 6 reading level. No jargon (AI-powered, automation, workflow, pipeline, CRM, integration). Say what happens: "we answer", "we book", "we follow up".
9. **White space and big type over boxes.** Fewer cards, borders and labels. Apple and Linear let one thing fill the screen at a time.
10. **Motion that explains, one at a time.** Keep the animations that show the work (the inbox, the charts). Never run two moving things in the same view, play once where you can, and always respect reduced motion.

## What we changed because of this

- Home page cut from 14 sections to 7, with one main button: "See what AI can do for you" (to the free Operations Map). WhatsApp stays as a small second option.
- Removed from home (still on inner pages): the ticker, the long offer block, the terms block, the client dashboard, the cost calculator, the proof list, the industries grid and the FAQ.
- The enquiry demo moved out of the hero into its own "Watch it work" section, so the hero has one moving thing.
- Copy rewritten for a 12-year-old across the home page, the Staff page, /audit and /services, and the shared industry page parts.
